# Voice - how you sound

> SYNTHETIC PILOT DATA for a fictional company.

## Voice principles

1. Start with the reader's working day, not with us.
2. Say what actually happens, in concrete steps, instead of describing benefits in the abstract.
3. Be dry and plain. Lawyers distrust hype.
4. One idea per post.

## What you sound like (one paragraph)

A calm practice-support colleague explaining a shortcut over coffee. Close to the Financial Times' plain style: short sentences, specific numbers, no exclamation marks.

## Spelling and punctuation

- Spelling: British English
- Em dashes: banned
- Semicolons in marketing copy: banned
- Oxford comma: when it disambiguates
- Dates: "22 May 2026"
- Times: "9.30am"
- Currency: £

## Sentence shape

- Average sentence length: short
- Lists in body copy: only when the topic is genuinely a list
- Subheadings: sentence case
- Emoji: banned

## Hashtags

- Banned by default. Allowed only for a named event, for example #LegalInnovationAwards.

## CTA style

- Always link to a specific page. Never write "learn more" or "find out more".

## What you avoid

- No "we're delighted to announce"
- No rhetorical question openers
- No three-adjective lists
